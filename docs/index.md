# The Science of Poker Study

A practical, **evidence-based** guide to actually getting better at poker — built on what cognitive science says about how skill is really learned, then applied directly to how you study the game.

!!! quote "The core idea"
    Most poker "study" is low-value: re-watching solver outputs, reviewing hands you already understand, grinding volume and hoping. The methods that *feel* productive are usually the ones that don't work. This wiki shows you the methods that **do** — and exactly how to apply them to solvers, drilling, databases, and your own game.

## The one-minute version

| Principle | What it means at the table/desk |
|---|---|
| **Test, don't review** | Drill spots *closed-book* (predict before you reveal the solver) — don't just read solutions |
| **Space it out** | Many short sessions beat marathons; use Anki for ranges & heuristics |
| **Mix it up** | Interleave spot types (random-spot drilling), don't grind one node 200× |
| **Trust the difficulty** | If study feels easy and smooth, you're probably not learning |
| **Extract the *why*** | Pull 3–5 heuristics from a sim; don't memorize mixed frequencies |
| **Test after a delay** | The honest check of what stuck — not how it felt while studying |
| **Process over results** | Variance means you can play perfectly and lose; judge decisions, not outcomes |
