# Putting It Together

The whole wiki, distilled into things you can actually do.

## The 10 principles, as actions

1. **Retrieve, don't review.** Replace re-reading with self-testing. Predict before you reveal. → [Retrieval Practice](retrieval-practice.md)
2. **Space it out.** Many short sessions over time beat one cram. Use expanding intervals (e.g. Anki). → [Spaced Practice](spaced-practice.md)
3. **Mix it up.** Once a topic is acquired, interleave it with related ones instead of blocking. → [Interleaving](interleaving.md)
4. **Trust the difficulty.** If it feels smooth and easy, be suspicious. Effortful = learning. → [Learning ≠ Performance](learning-vs-performance.md)
5. **Match difficulty to your level.** New & complex → study worked examples first. Already competent → generate and test. → [Cognitive Load](cognitive-load.md)
6. **Practice deliberately.** Target a specific weakness at the edge of your ability, with feedback. Quality over hours. → [Deliberate Practice](deliberate-practice.md)
7. **Reason out the *why*.** Don't accept spoon-fed answers; explain the principle to yourself. → [Feedback](feedback-and-metacognition.md)
8. **Test after a delay to check what stuck.** Never judge your learning while looking at the answer. → [Self-Awareness](feedback-and-metacognition.md)
9. **Protect attention.** One task, finished, at a time. Strain for learning, flow for performing. → [Attention & Flow](attention-and-flow.md)
10. **Engineer transfer & protect sleep.** Abstract principles + varied practice; sleep to consolidate. → [Transfer & Sleep](transfer-and-sleep.md)

## A simple weekly loop you can copy

!!! example "The loop"
    1. **Pick one weakness** to work on (specific, at your edge).
    2. **Learn it** — if new/complex, study a worked example first; understand the *why*.
    3. **Drill it by retrieval** — closed-book, predict-before-reveal.
    4. **Capture the principle** as a spaced-repetition card (Anki).
    5. **Test it cold after a delay** (next session / next week) — the honest check.
    6. **Apply it** in varied, realistic situations; get feedback.
    7. **Space and interleave** it with older topics over the following weeks.
    8. **Sleep** on it.

## What to stop doing

- ❌ Re-reading / re-watching / highlighting as your main method
- ❌ Cramming one topic in a marathon
- ❌ Memorizing answers without the *why*
- ❌ Judging "I know this" while looking at the solution
- ❌ Multitasking through study
- ❌ Trading sleep for a few more fatigued hours

## The one-sentence summary

> **Test yourself, space it out, mix it up, reason the *why*, check cold after a delay, focus on one thing, and sleep on it — and trust the difficulty, because the comfortable methods are the ones that don't work.**
